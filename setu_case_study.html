Fol. 04
The Approach

Smooth the income before the person has to

Instead of asking gig workers to budget against a paycheck they don't have, Setu does the smoothing computationally — and only ever shows one confident number.

01

One number, not a spreadsheet

Every income app defaults to charts and categories. Gig workers told researchers they want one thing: can I afford this right now. Setu's home screen answers only that — a "safe to spend today" figure, computed from a rolling average of real earnings against known upcoming bills.

02

Save on the good days, automatically

Rather than a fixed monthly SIP a salaried app would suggest, Setu sets aside a small percentage only on above-average earning days — so saving never competes with a bad week's rent money.

03

Turn consistency into credit access

No payslip means no loan eligibility today. Setu converts saving consistency into a visible "Trust Score" gig workers can eventually show lenders — addressing the credit-access gap the research flagged directly.

Fol. 06
Design Decisions

A few choices worth explaining

Why a ledger entry, not a progress ring?

Circular gauges are the default fintech pattern, but they don't mean anything to someone who has never used a budgeting app. A ledger-line number — amount, underline, label — mirrors the daily hisab format workers already keep by hand.

Why maroon and brass, not fintech green?

Green in finance UI almost always signals stock-market growth, which doesn't apply here. Maroon and brass are drawn from ledger-book binding and ink — warmer, more everyday, less "invest now."

Why a light interface for an outdoor-first app?

Dark mode looks premium in a portfolio, but most gig workers check earnings on a cracked mid-range screen in direct sunlight. High-contrast warm paper reads better outdoors than a dark theme does.

Why Hindi micro-copy alongside English?

The primary number on the home screen is bilingual by default because the target user may be more comfortable reading spoken-style Hindi than financial English — the interface should adapt to them, not the other way round.

Fol. 07
Honest Reflection

What this is — and isn't — yet

Setu is a self-initiated concept, designed to explore a real, well-documented problem I don't have first-hand professional experience solving. The problem framing is grounded in published labour and financial-inclusion research (cited above), not in original interviews I conducted myself — I want to be upfront about that distinction.

The obvious next step: running 5–8 short interviews with actual delivery or ride-hail partners to pressure-test the "safe to spend" logic and the Trust Score concept before treating either as validated. I'd rather present this as a considered starting point than overstate it as tested.
